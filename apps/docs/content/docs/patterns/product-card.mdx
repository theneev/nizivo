## Notes

- **One link, one Tab stop.** Each card is an `<article>` whose title is a real `<a>`. The link's
  `::after` is stretched over the card, so the whole card is the hit area and shows the focus ring,
  but the link's name stays the product's title. Nothing else in the card is interactive: a
  quick-add or wishlist button inside a link is a nested control, so put it beside the card, not in
  it. Making the whole card an `<a>` would read every word on it as the link's name.
- **Status in words.** The design's Status axis mixes publication (Active, Draft) with inventory (Sold
  out), and the example table shows a fourth state, Low stock, that the card has no variant for.
  The example derives one Badge from both facts and says the inventory in a line of its own; the
  Badge's dot is decorative.
- **Sale prices.** The struck-through price before a sale is read by screen readers without its
  strike-through, so each price has a visually hidden word ("Sale price", "Regular price"). Both are
  `NumberValue` with the store's currency.
- **Long titles** wrap; the price keeps its line and its column. The design's title has no truncation
  and would collide with the price.
- **Images.** The grey block, a square, stands in for the product's
  [`Image`](/docs/components/image/), with `alt=""` because the title next to it names the product.
  Its radius is the card's radius less its padding, so the corners stay concentric (the design's
  14 px is off the scale).
- **Contrast.** The design's inventory line uses `text/tertiary`, 4.31:1 on white. The example uses
  `text/secondary`, and Nizivo's `text/tertiary` is re-pointed to pass anyway.
